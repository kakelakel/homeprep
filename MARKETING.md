# HomePrep Marketing & Growth Roadmap

HomePrep is not marketed as another inventory tracker.

It is marketed as a **local-first preparedness system that helps households stay ready over time**.

> **Know what you have. Know what needs attention. Be ready.**
>
> **Your preparedness. Your infrastructure. Your data.**

This document defines the marketing, community-growth and long-term monetization direction for HomePrep.

---

## 1. Positioning

### The problem

Preparedness is easy to plan once and surprisingly hard to maintain.

Food expires. Water needs rotation. Batteries die. Equipment goes unchecked. Emergency bags drift out of date. Household members forget procedures. Printed checklists become stale.

HomePrep exists to make preparedness a maintained household system rather than a one-time project.

### Core message

**HomePrep helps households know what they have, where it is, what needs attention and what to do next.**

### Product principles worth marketing

- Local-first.
- Self-hostable.
- No mandatory HomePrep cloud account.
- Home Assistant remains a first-class deployment mode.
- User-owned preparedness data.
- Designed to remain useful during degraded connectivity.
- Practical maintenance rather than fear-based prepping.

### Target audiences

Primary audiences:

1. Home Assistant users.
2. Self-hosting enthusiasts.
3. Preparedness / prepper communities.
4. Privacy-conscious households.
5. Homeowners who maintain emergency supplies and safety equipment.

Later audiences:

6. Families that want a simple standalone preparedness tool without Home Assistant.
7. Community groups, housing associations and small organizations.
8. Professional preparedness and facilities-management users.

---

## 2. Current launch stage

HomePrep is currently in the **discovery and validation** stage.

The goal is not revenue yet.

The goal is to build:

- real users
- trust
- installation feedback
- feature feedback
- screenshots and real-world examples
- GitHub activity
- community recognition
- a clear product story

### North-star metric for this stage

**Active households using HomePrep**

GitHub stars and social engagement are useful discovery indicators, but actual installations and returning users matter more.

# 3. 90-day growth roadmap

## Phase 1 — Foundation
### Weeks 1–2

Goal: make every visitor immediately understand HomePrep.

Actions:

- Keep the GitHub README strongly product-oriented.
- Put the value proposition before technical detail.
- Maintain clear screenshots of Overview, Inventory, Containers, Assets, Plans and Lovelace cards.
- Keep installation instructions short and prominent.
- Maintain one permanent Home Assistant Community thread.
- Maintain one primary Reddit launch thread and use meaningful development updates instead of repetitive promotion.
- Add contribution guidance when outside contributors begin appearing.
- Add a simple project website / landing page when worthwhile.

Content to publish:

- Why HomePrep exists.
- A 60–90 second product walkthrough.
- "What happens when your emergency supplies quietly expire?"
- Containers / Go Bag workflow.
- Local-first architecture explanation.
- Printable/offline preparedness direction.

Success indicators:

- first external bug reports
- first external feature requests
- first repeat commenters
- GitHub stars growing without direct asks
- users reporting successful installations

---

## Phase 2 — Community expansion
### Weeks 3–6

Goal: reach adjacent communities without becoming spammy.

Priority channels:

1. Home Assistant Community
2. r/homeassistant
3. r/selfhosted
4. preparedness / prepper communities
5. relevant Home Assistant Discord/community spaces where self-promotion is allowed
6. YouTube Shorts / short demo videos

Each channel gets content adapted to its audience.

### Home Assistant angle

Lead with:

- native integration
- Lovelace cards
- notifications
- recurring tasks
- local-first data
- no collection of helpers/templates required

### Self-hosted angle

Lead with:

- HomePrep Server
- Windows / Docker / Linux / NAS direction
- portability
- backups
- user-owned database
- optional remote access rather than mandatory cloud

### Preparedness angle

Lead with:

- expiring supplies
- water rotation
- go bags / containers
- family plans
- equipment inspections
- printable/offline information
- readiness gaps

Do not lead with Home Assistant jargon outside the Home Assistant audience.

## Phase 3 — Content engine
### Weeks 7–12

Goal: make HomePrep discoverable even when no release is happening.

Recurring content pillars:

### A. Problem → solution

Examples:

- "Your emergency food isn't useful if half of it expired two years ago."
- "When did you last inspect the fire extinguisher?"
- "A Go Bag isn't ready just because a bag exists."
- "Preparedness data should still be yours when the Internet is down."

### B. Feature spotlight

One feature at a time:

- Containers
- Assets
- Tasks
- Plans
- Targets
- Shopping List
- Readiness Overview
- Notifications
- Offline exports
- Home Assistant cards

### C. Build in public

Show why product decisions were made.

Examples:

- why Containers became broader than Go Bags
- why Targets are editable after adopting Guidance
- why empty readiness areas are excluded from the score
- why HomePrep refuses mandatory centralized storage

### D. Community-driven development

When a community suggestion becomes functionality:

> You asked for X. HomePrep now does X.

This is especially valuable because it demonstrates that feedback changes the product.

### E. Preparedness knowledge

Evergreen educational content that can naturally lead to HomePrep:

- household water rotation
- emergency lighting
- document backups
- evacuation bags
- power-outage planning
- household shutoff documentation
- recurring safety inspections

Avoid alarmist or fear-driven marketing.

# 4. Publishing cadence

A sustainable default cadence:

### Every week

- 1 useful community / development post
- 1 feature or problem/solution post
- respond to active Reddit and HA Community discussions
- review GitHub activity

### Every significant release

Create a release marketing pack containing:

- GitHub release summary
- Home Assistant Community update
- Reddit update where appropriate
- short social post
- one screenshot or short demo
- 30–60 second video script

### Every month

Publish a broader project update:

- what shipped
- what users requested
- what changed because of feedback
- what comes next

---

# 5. Content rules

HomePrep marketing should feel like development communication, not advertising.

### Do

- demonstrate real use cases
- show screenshots
- explain design decisions
- credit useful community feedback
- be transparent about unfinished areas
- invite testing
- talk about resilience and maintenance
- emphasize data ownership

### Do not

- spam identical release announcements across communities
- manufacture urgency
- use disaster fear as a sales tactic
- claim users are "safe" because they use HomePrep
- imply that HomePrep replaces official emergency guidance
- hide core functionality behind a paywall after users adopt it

---

# 6. Conversion path

The desired discovery path is:

```text
Interesting post / search result
        ↓
GitHub or HomePrep website
        ↓
Understand HomePrep in <30 seconds
        ↓
See screenshots / demo
        ↓
Install
        ↓
Complete onboarding
        ↓
Create useful preparedness data
        ↓
Return because HomePrep surfaces something that needs attention
        ↓
Recommend HomePrep / contribute / support project
```

Every marketing decision should make one of these transitions easier.

# 7. Metrics

Track weekly where practical:

## Discovery

- GitHub stars
- GitHub forks
- repository visitors / clones when available
- Home Assistant Community views and replies
- Reddit votes, comments and referral traffic
- website traffic when a website exists

## Adoption

- release downloads where meaningful
- HACS availability and installs if obtainable
- standalone installer downloads
- successful setup reports

## Engagement

- issues opened
- feature requests
- returning commenters
- contributors
- community-created screenshots / configs / translations

## Product health

- bug reports after releases
- onboarding confusion
- frequently requested features
- upgrade failures
- installation friction

Do not optimize only for social engagement. The useful outcome is households actually maintaining preparedness with HomePrep.

# 9. Monetization strategy

Monetization should begin only after HomePrep has demonstrated sustained usage and trust.

## Permanent rule

**HomePrep Core remains useful without a paid HomePrep cloud service.**

Core local/self-hosted functionality should not be deliberately crippled to force subscriptions.

### Stage 1 — Voluntary support

Possible options:

- GitHub Sponsors
- Ko-fi / Buy Me a Coffee
- donations

Introduce this only when people begin asking how they can support the project or when ongoing project costs justify it.

### Stage 2 — Supporter program

Possible benefits:

- supporter badge
- development previews
- roadmap voting
- beta access
- cosmetic themes
- supporter community channel

Do not reserve essential preparedness functionality for supporters.

### Stage 3 — Paid convenience

Potential products:

- polished mobile clients
- optional managed encrypted remote-access relay
- hosted convenience services
- managed backup service

These should complement self-hosting rather than replace it.

### Stage 4 — Hardware

Potential **HomePrep Hub**:

- small preconfigured appliance
- local HomePrep Server
- simple onboarding
- aimed at households that do not want to administer a server

### Stage 5 — Organizations

Potential future HomePrep organization edition:

- multiple locations
- shared responsibilities
- audit / inspection history
- role-based access
- organizational preparedness dashboards

Possible users include community groups, small organizations, housing organizations and facilities teams.

This should remain separate from the household product where enterprise requirements would otherwise distort HomePrep Core.

# 10. Monetization triggers

Do not monetize merely because a feature can be monetized.

Good triggers include:

- meaningful recurring active usage
- users independently recommending HomePrep
- regular support requests
- infrastructure costs becoming material
- users asking for easier hosted/mobile access
- organizations asking for multi-site functionality

A useful milestone model:

### Early community

0–1,000 users

Focus entirely on product quality, distribution and trust.

### Established project

1,000–10,000 users

Evaluate donations, sponsorship and supporter options.

### Product ecosystem

10,000+ users or strong recurring demand

Evaluate managed services, mobile monetization, hardware and organizational offerings.

User count is directional rather than a strict gate.

## Final principle

The strongest HomePrep marketing is a product that visibly solves real preparedness problems and earns recommendations from users.

Growth should therefore follow this loop:

```text
Listen → Build → Show → Learn → Improve → Repeat
```

HomePrep should become known less because it advertises aggressively and more because it is repeatedly useful.
